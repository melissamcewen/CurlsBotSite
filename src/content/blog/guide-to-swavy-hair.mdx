Is my hair just straight? Or could it be wavy? If you've ever wondered this, you might have swavy hair.

Let's get this straight (haha), if you had genuinely straight hair, this would not be a question on your mind.

You my friend, have swavy hair. Swavy is one of the most misunderstood hair types. I’ve seen it dismissed as “barely wavy” or “almost straight.”

The truth is your hair IS swavy, it's loose waves. And while it might never be curls, it is surprisingly versatile and beautiful in its own way.

## TL;DR

Swavy hair is NOT straight hair. It is a loose wavy hair type. Go for lightweight products and generally avoid heavy creams and oils. My general formula for a routine is volumizing shampoo, volumizing conditioner, a light spray leave-in, and a light mousse OR gel.

## What Is Swavy Hair

<BlogImage
  src="/images/hair-types/photos/swavy.png"
  alt="Photo of Swavy hair"
  caption="Some examples of swavy hair"
  float="right"
/>

In the [CurlsBot type system](/hair-types), **Swavy hair** is hair that:

- Does not naturally dry completely straight with zero bends or waves
- Also does not ever form curls, even occasionally

It is the most elongated type in [our system](/hair-types). Physics-wise, it’s still a coil, just an extremely elongated one. Shrinkage is also a huge part of our system, which is the difference between the length of hair in its dry resting state and its stretched length. For swavy hair, the shrinkage is extremely small.

You’ll sometimes see this texture labeled as “1b” or “1c” on TikTok or modern charts, but those labels are derived from the Walker system. In the original Walker system, _any_ visible wave was type 2, and 1c referred to coarse straight hair that is usually extremely straight and resistant to texture. So those labels don’t really map cleanly onto what people are trying to describe now. There is a hair type system called the Irrizarry system that has a "beach waves" type that is similar to swavy, but this system isn't commonly used. [You can read more in my post on the science and history of hair typing systems.](/blog/the-science-of-hair-typing)

I did not invent the term “swavy” and do not know who did, but I do know the s is there because loose waves often form an s pattern.

## How Do You Know If You Have Swavy Hair

The easiest answer is: take the CurlsBot quiz.

<HairTypeQuizBanner />

But conceptually, it’s simple. CurlsBot curl typing is based on scientific evidence that straight hair is structurally distinct from other textures. You can read more about it in our guide to the science of hair typing.

If your hair:

- Does not dry completely, totally straight
- Has some bends, flips, or soft waves when dried naturally
- Never (or almost never) curls

That’s Swavy.

If your hair is often described as “straight but frizzy” or “straight but poofy,” it may also be Swavy.

Hair type can also change due to hormones and other factors. Many people who have swavy hair report their hair was straight previously. It's also not uncommon for people to go from swavy to wavy, with a stronger wave pattern and occasional curls. 


## How Swavy Differs From Other Hair Types

<BlogImage
  src="/images/blog/guide-to-swavy-hair/Patterns.png"
  alt="Illustration of some different swavy hair patterns"
  caption="Some examples of swavy hair patterns"
  float="right"
/>

**Compared to straight hair:**
Swavy hair does not dry perfectly straight and is more prone to frizz and puffiness. But on the plus side many straight people struggle to add texture, and adding some texture is a bit easier with swavy hair.

**Compared to wavy hair:**
Wavy hair, in our system, sits closer to curls. It’s mostly waves but may occasionally curl. Swavy never does.

Swavy is usually lumped into categories like “1a–2a,” “2a–2c,” or the LOIS “S” type, which is part of why it gets so muddled.

Hair type is also fluid. If Swavy hair changes types, it is most likely to change to wavy.

I currently have Swavy hair and used to have wavy hair. In my case, the shift was likely driven by hormones, aging, and my haircut.

## Subtypes of Swavy Hair

Not all swavy hair is the same! Hair type is just one factor that can influence how your hair behaves. There are other factors that can influence how your hair behaves such as:

<HairTypeFactors />
- **Strand width**

  - **Fine** (small strand width) swavy hair is prone to static flyaways and low volume, so benefits from anti-static products and texture-adding products
  - **Coarse** (large strand width) swavy hair is less static-prone but can have more stubborn frizz. Coarse hair is less easily weighed down than fine hair, so this type can usually use heavier products without worrying as much about weighing down hair. 

- **Density**

  - **Thin** (low density) swavy hair looks greasy very easily
  - **Thick** (high density) swavy hair may tolerate slightly richer products, but still not heavy ones

- **Porosity**
  - Swavy hair can be any porosity
  - [**High porosity**](/porosity/high-porosity) Swavy hair is tricky because damage is often mistaken for a need for heavy products
  - Lightweight formulas with cationic ingredients that bind to damaged areas can work better than rich creams for **high porosity** swavy hair, as they provide protection/smoothing without adding as much weight

Most people have a mix of all of the above. I have fine _and_ coarse strands, thin _and_ thick areas, higher-porosity ends, and lower-porosity roots.

That’s normal. [If you're curious about your porosity take our porosity quiz](/porosity-quiz/).

## The Science of Swavy Hair

A few relevant points from the research:

- Looser textures show more pattern variability
- L’Oréal’s system includes a type 2 that is not straight but not very wavy, which likely maps well to Swavy
- From a physics perspective, Swavy hair behaves like an extremely elongated coil
- The **conditioning trade-off curve** for Swavy hair is very low, meaning it’s easy to lose volume with excess conditioning

You can read more about this in our posts about our [curl type system](/blog/curlsbot-curl-typing) and [the science and history of hair typing](/blog/the-science-of-hair-typing).

## Styling and Styling Products for Swavy Hair

First you need to think about what you want to achieve with your hair. Do you want:

- To [maximize your wave](#maximize-the-wave)? 
- Just [minimize the frizz](#minimize-the-frizz) and not worry about maximizing waves?
- [Add some texture/volume](#adding-texture-volume) to make your hair look less flat?

### Maximize the wave

If you want to maximize your wave pattern the main principles are
- More hold = more wave
- Less weight = more wave
- Don't do stuff that loosens your wave

Your goal it to add enough product to enhance the hold, but not so much that it weighs your hair down. And once you have the wave you want, to keep it wavy by protecting it and not brushing it. 

Your product options are
- Mousse: Easy for beginners to apply
- Gel: A bit harder to use but more hold in general
- Hairspray: Usually used to on dry hair to keep the style in place
- SOME (but not most) creams: Most creams are too heavy for swavy hair, but there are a few on the market with 

The problem with hold products is can be hard to find the right one and also hard to learn how to use them. It's normal to try even like 3 gels and be like "ugh gels are not for me." But I can almost guarantee there is a gel or mousse out there that will work for you. [Curls Monthly](https://curlsmonthly.com/?ref=curlsbot) provides sample sizes of a lot of gels and mousses, or you can go to your local store and buy travel size gels/mousses to try. 

In general I think these products work best on soaking wet hair (I apply IN the shower right after rinsing conditioner). I'd start there, and start with a small amount and keep trying larger amounts until you have the amount of hold you need.

Remember WATER is weight, if your hair is wet while it's drying that's A LOT of weight and will stretch out your waves.
- Scrunch with a tee or hair towel to remove water and enhance waves
- Diffuse to remove water and enhance pattern

If you air dry you have to accept that your waves will not be at their max. Personally I diffuse for 10 minutes because I don't like diffusing, then air dry the rest of way. It gives me a good balance between maximizing wave and not spending time on something I dislike and honestly even like a few minutes of diffusing can make a huge difference. Also keep in mind that diffusing is a technique, it's hard to get it right the first time. Watch videos on diffusing and be patient as you learn.  

You also need to be realistic about weight of the hair itself. **Long hair will never have maximum wave**. You can consider layers or just accept that looser waves can be beautiful too. 

For preserving the waves once you have them
- Consider locking them in with hairspray (though TBH I hate how most hairspray feels so I don't do this often)
- DON'T BRUSH THEM DRY, this will loosen your wave A LOT
- Protect your hair overnight with a bonnet/high ponytail (sometimes called "pineappling")

If the waves fall out by the 2nd day, you can often reactivate your products by re-wetting your hair, scrunching it, and diffusing a bit. This is too time-consuming for me so I just use a [refresh spray like Curl Keeper's](https://link.amazon/B05PXKw4U) to wet it a tiny bit and then scrunch – the results aren't as strong, but that refresh spray also adds a bit of texture so I can get a nice "beach waves" look. 
 
 ### Minimize the frizz

This is for those of us who don't want to chase after enhancing waves and are OK with a nice loose slight wave, we just don't want the frizz/poofiness. And also don't want greasiness and oiliness.

For minimizing frizz you'll want to use anti-frizz products, and these are usually not curly/wavy products so if you're used to just buying curly products, you'll have to look outside the box. Some suggestions:

- A spray leave in like [Pantene 10 in 1](https://amzn.to/4oYxgGf)
- An "oil" specifically formulated to be light like [Verb ghost oil](https://amzn.to/48KVOOl)

For me, my spray leave-in pretty much does the job. I sometimes add more of it when styling dry.

Brushing *can* be a part of this. I use a [boar bristle brush](https://link.amazon/B0fVjecMI) or goat hair brush. If you're getting poofiness when you brush, it means you need more anti-frizz product like a spray leave in or light oil. 

Dry shampoo can also be a big help, for both adding volume at the roots and keeping greasiness/oiliness at bay. I like [R+CO Badlands Dry Shampoo Paste](https://link.amazon/A04HU1xTv) for this. 

Then there is heat, I don't have that much experience with this, but heat stying with a blowout or hair straightener can be very effective. Just make sure to use heat protection and remember these are skills that can take time to master. My guide to the [Abbey Yung method](/blog/the-abbey-yung-method) may be helpful here because her routine is centered around heat.

## Products to use with caution: oils, creams, deep conditioners

The main products I see causing issues for swavy hair are:
- Oils
- Creams
- Deep conditioners

These all add weight to your hair and can loosen out the pattern. That doesn't mean all products of these types are bad, you can find oils, creams, and deep conditioners specifically formulated to be light. You'll also want to think about what role these products play in your routine.

**Deep conditioner**: Not everyone needs these, they are most useful if you have coarse hair and you want to soften it

**Creams**: Hair creams are ultra confusing because there are actually two types
- Ones that are basically just leave in conditioner. My favorite light one is [Curl Wow Flo-etry](https://amzn.to/4pLBQJr), I add it to areas of my hair that tend to feel dry especially in the winter. 
- Hybrid leave-in/soft hold styler: These offer some extra conditioning plus a soft hold. They aren't going to maximize your waves, but if you prefer a soft loose wavy look they can be great. For example [Kitsch Smoothing Air-Dry Cream](https://amzn.to/4s1uxP7), which I sometimes use while traveling bc then I don't have to pack a separate leave-in and styler. My hair does look less wavy with this b the ingredients are heavier and the hold is softer, but it also doesn't look frizzy.


**Oils**: Another confusing product because there are also two types!
- Plain oil: This is just plain old fashioned oil, like the coconut or olive oil in your kitchen. Use these with caution because even a little too much can weigh your hair down a lot and cause it to look greasy. 
- Blends: Blends of oils and other ingredients, specifically engineered to provide the benefits of oil without the excess weight. If your spray leave in isn't reducing frizz enough, especially when brushing, these can be useful. If you're going for max waviness, avoid these. 
  - In conventional products, those other ingredients are usually silicones. An example of a lightweight silicone oil blend is [Verb Ghost Oil](https://amzn.to/48KVOOl). 
  - In more "natural" products they may be an ester like caprylic/capric triglyceride, which is derived from natural oils but provides the shine/slip without as much greasiness. For example [Odele Hair Oil](https://link.amazon/B0bFvCH4X).

## Signs you have the wrong products

Swavy hair is very prone to **overconditioning**, which is when hair feels limp, mushy, and often has a weaker wave or curl pattern.

Some people mistake this for "moisture overload," but it has more to do with conditioners than it does with moisture. It happens when too many heavy conditioning products weigh your hair strands down, turning them mushy or stringy.

The solution? Clarify and lighter products!

## The Great Brush Debate

If you're online you'll often encounter advice that says NEVER DRY BRUSH WAVY HAIR, but I brush mine often. It depends on your goals. 

If you don't mind stretching out/elongating your waves, brushing can be a great way to smooth and style your hair. You can read more about this is my  [minimize the frizz](#minimize-the-frizz) section. 


## Haircuts for Swavy Hair

My tips for haircuts for swavy hair, as someone asking for a good one:

**Most curly cuts are not worth it**.  I've had Devacuts and Ouidad and I think the training for these does not really address loose waves well enough for people like us to benefit. I think there are some individual stylists that are skilled at using dry cuts (the same base technique) on loose waves, but there's no certification/training for it that I know of yet, so you'll have to shop around. 

**When looking for a new stylist check out their Instagram/website/etc** and see if they have any posts that show work on loose waves. This is very much related to the previous tip.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                

**With long hair you're going to have to accept that the weight of your hair does loosen your pattern**. Very long hair = mostly accepting your hair will have a very loose wave. 

**Layers can act weird**, with medium length pieces springing up and then the longer pieces looking almost straight -- especially on day 2 hair. When my hair is very long, I like to ask for cuts that focus on some face framing at the front and shaping at the back. 

**Less length usually means more pattern BUT there are exceptions**. Like at very very short my hair is curly, after that it becomes wavy, then at medium it looks almost straight, and finally when long it just has a ringlet pattern at the ends.

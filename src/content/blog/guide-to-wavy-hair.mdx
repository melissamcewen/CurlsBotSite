“Frizz is just a curl waiting to come out.” You may have seen that phrase tossed around online. But if your hair is nearly curly, usually frizzy, and you “never knew you had curls,” then you are most likely a wavy like me!

I never really understood my hair type until I had my babies. Two of them are extremely curly and that trait comes from my side of the family. That was my lightbulb moment to think, “*what if my hair is actually textured?*”

The first place I realized I had some form of a curl pattern was at the nape of my neck. When I put my hair into a high bun, I would have tiny curls at the nape of my neck. So cute! But so frustrating because they appeared nowhere else.  When I started paying closer attention to my hair type, I also noticed that the “stubborn frizzy” areas right at my temples were in fact waves that I was relentlessly slicking back to tame.

Bottom line is, if you’re borderline curly, but get called out by the curl police online, then your hair is probably wavy. If you're not sure, [CurlsBot has a handy quiz](/hair-types/quiz) that can help you distinguish between the three related [loose curl types](/hair-types)): [swavy](/blog/guide-to-swavy-hair/), wavy, and loose curls.

## How wavy differs from other hair types

When left to air dry, wavy hair definitely still has a recognizable pattern to it. When using heavier products, it will still bounce or frizz up a bit, unlike a looser wave pattern (["swavy"](/blog/guide-to-swavy-hair/)) that might appear nearly to fully straight when air drying or heavy with product.

When properly clarified, conditioned, and styled with mousse or gel and then diffused, wavy hair looks nearly curly at the tips, and more straight at the root. Some wave patterns go almost to the root, while some are a few inches of straighter looking hair from the top. 

Wavy hair can be a mix of “s waves” to actual ringlets. I have a bit of both in my hair, even without product or diffusing. 

Wavy hair is pretty easy to straighten with a straightening iron (always heat protect though!) but also easily takes on the shape of however it dries. In my humble opinion, it’s the best of both worlds, whether going for a sleek look or borderline curly.

## How to Care for Wavy Hair

### Wash Frequency 
*Wavy pro-tip: Don't be afraid to wash your hair*

Wash frequency should  depend upon how your scalp feels. There is absolutely no trophy for going a week without washing your hair. Likewise, you might not need to wash daily. Ignore any rule books about this and judge your wash day frequency by how your scalp is feeling. For reference, I wash my hair about 3 times per week. Sometimes more often, sometimes less.

### Step 1: Clarifying & Cleansing

*Wavy pro-tip: Don't skip a clarifying shampoo! Cleaner hair can = more waves*

Most importantly, do not be afraid to shampoo with a clarifying or chelating cleanser once per week or even more often if needed. 

So much information online cautions against clarifying shampoos, but this applies to hair that is curlier than ours. Wavy hair can handle the strong cleansers, and many are labeled as being color safe, in case you’re wondering.

The most important part of your wash day will be the shampoo you choose. A clarifying shampoo that also removes hard water mineral buildup (like [Odele's Clarifying shampoo](https://link.amazon/B0bITwHqz)) will get you off to a great start. My absolute favorite is [K18 Detox Peptide Prep shampoo](https://amzn.to/40YyNDe). It is worth the hype, trust! Another highly effective option is [Malibu C Hard Water Wellness](https://link.amazon/B0dL6FHmY).

### Step 2: Conditioning & Detangling

**Wavy Pro-tip**: Looking for a lighter conditioner? Try using a leave-in conditioner by itself

If your hair is fairly easy to detangle, did you know that you can either:

A. Use a leave-in conditioner in place of a regular conditioner. Rinse it out a bit just like a regular conditioner. OR

B. Just skip regular conditioner and use a leave-in conditioner on its own 

This is my favorite hack for maximum volume and bounce on my wash day. Stylers provide enough conditioning for my waves because I tend to use gels and/or mousses that are labeled for “curls.”

Some great leave-in options include [EnLove Beauty Miracle X Bond Repair Leave-in Conditioner](https://link.amazon/B06vq9d9P), Not Your [Mother’s Curl Talk Leave-in conditioner](https://link.amazon/B0doeLAQd), and [TreLuxe Untie the Knot leave-in conditioner](https://link.amazon/B0iPYdbc2). Plus the TreLuxe one smells like Christmastime!

If your hair is difficult to detangle, a regular conditioner or even a mask is a suitable option after thoroughly clarifying. Always follow the instructions on the product label and rinse out completely if it says so. None of this “leave a bit of conditioner in your hair” has been helpful to my wavy hair, and can lead to over conditioning.

If your hair likes a traditional rinse out conditioner, I recommend [Rizos Curls Gloss and Repair mask](https://link.amazon/B0ecojlN1) or [Bounce Curl Weightless Conditioning mask](https://link.amazon/B07eICiuj).

### The Foundation: Mousse First

I always start with mousse and recommend you do too. Don't be afraid to use a lot! I apply it to very wet hair right in the shower and finger comb or brush it through all of my hair from root to tip. Then I’ll scrunch it all in, microplop (a type of scrunching technique, see [video](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Df2yCikKi9G/?cnvx=MTM5ZHBzNnY1czJ5MA==)), and add a bit more mousse for good measure. For every handful of hair I have, I use one palmful of mousse. (For me, that’s 3 in total.)

Here are my top faves (some are foams which are basically the same thing): [Miche Set Gel-to-Foam](https://link.amazon/B0gcZzydf), [Tresemme Bouncy Curls](https://link.amazon/B0gR6KW8P), [Aussie Instant Freeze Mousse](https://link.amazon/B0gGpujhd), and [EnLove Beauty Moisture Crush Foaming Mousse](https://link.amazon/B02VALE1m).

### Locking It In: Layering Gel

You can totally go onto diffusing from there, but if you’re wanting longevity and a bit more hold and added shine, layering on a gel can be very useful. I glaze on about three fingertips worth of gel for each palmful of mousse that I used (so for me, that’s 3 fingertips’ worth, three times.)

Other wavies swear by gel as a single styler. This gives a nice, defined result that you can expect to last for one to four days (or more!). I love beginning with a mousse, then layering gel for maximum volume and definition. My style lasts 2 to 4 days between washes.

## A basic wavy hair routine 

Start with a clarifying or chelating (hard water shampoo) wash and rinse and repeat if your hair feels gummy or has product buildup.

  src="/images/blog/guide-to-wavy-hair/scrunch.png"

Choose a lightweight conditioner that says something like “Volumizing” on the label and apply it to your waves. I also like to gently detangle at this stage, though sometimes my hair has already been detangled before washing. 

On soaking wet hair, use your fingers to rake through a mousse and then scrunch.

Use a t shirt to scrunch out excess water (this is sometimes called "microplopping", see [a video here](https://www.instagram.com/reel/Df2yCikKi9G/?cnvx=MTM5ZHBzNnY1czJ5MA==)).

If you’d like to add a touch more of mousse or a bit of gel, now is the time to add it.

Air dry for ease or diffuse for best results that last longer (and are bouncier).


## Drying and Refreshing

I know it can feel like it takes forever and a day, but I always diffuse my waves. If not, I’ve got a straight hair look, which is not my preference.

Microplopping, or using a t shirt to scrunch out some water prior to diffusing is especially helpful in speeding up dry time and preserving definition. You can also use a microfiber towel if you like.

I have found that typical curly hair refreshing routines don’t work for my hair type. Instead, I make sure to protect my hair overnight for sleep, and then just let it be for about two days or so. On day 3, I will usually dry brush and add a dab of product to tame any frizz. Some wavies avoid dry brushing but my hair loves it. Always go with what feels best for your hair.

## Preserving your waves overnight

Bonnets, silk pillowcases, and scrunchies are often recommended for this hair type. I prefer a plain cotton t shirt on dry hair for sleep ([check out this video for how to tie it](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfyQ15mKX9r/)). It keeps my super slippery hair tucked in neatly and won’t budge (much). Without this, my hair does not look very wavy in the morning and needs to be rewashed.
